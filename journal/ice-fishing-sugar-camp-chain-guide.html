First ice on the Sugar Camp Chain

When the chain freezes, how to judge the ice, what bites first, and where to start on water this big.

JournalArea GuideWhere to EatFishingThe Cabins

There is a strange few weeks every year when the Northwoods belongs to nobody. The boats are out, the snowmobiles are not running yet, and the Sugar Camp Chain sits there going quiet and glassy while everyone waits. Then the ice comes, the first holes get drilled, and the panfish bite like they have been waiting too. Here is how we think about first ice out our back door — and what to know before you walk out on it.

Timing

When does the Sugar Camp Chain actually freeze?

Ice-up here is a process, not a date. The shallow, sheltered bays skin over first, usually while the main basins are still open and steaming on cold mornings. A run of still, clear, cold nights is what builds hard black ice fast; wind and a warm afternoon undo a surprising amount of it. That is why two winters a week apart on the calendar can look nothing alike on the water.

The thing that makes this chain different from a single lake is the current. Sugar Camp is a string of lakes joined by narrows and channels, and moving water keeps ice thin long after the bays are safe. If you only remember one sentence from this guide, make it that one. Our ice-up guide walks through the same freeze pattern on the Eagle River Chain next door.

Safety

How do you judge the ice before you trust it?

Locals do not guess, and they do not trust what the ice looked like last weekend. They drill. A spud bar or an auger and a tape measure, checked as you walk out, is the whole method — clear ice is strong, cloudy or honeycombed ice is not, and slush on top means water is working underneath. Stay well off any narrows, inlet, spring hole or dock bubbler, go with somebody, wear a float suit or carry picks, and tell a person at the cabin where you are headed.

Nobody can tell you from a keyboard whether the ice is safe the day you arrive. Ask at a bait shop in town, look at where the local shacks are sitting, and if the answer is vague, that is your answer.

The bite

What is biting under first ice?

Panfish first

Bluegill, crappie and perch stack in the green weeds of the shallow bays right at first ice. Small jigs, waxies or plastics, and a light rod — this is the most family-friendly fishing of the whole winter.

Walleye at the edges

Work the deeper weed edges and breaklines at dawn and dusk. A jigging spoon on one line and a minnow on a deadstick a few feet away covers both moods.

Northern pike on tip-ups

Set tip-ups with sizeable minnows over a weedy bay flat and let them work while you jig. Pike are the reason first ice never gets boring out here.

Seasons and limits are set by the Wisconsin DNR and they do change, so check the current regulations for Oneida and Vilas counties before you drill. Musky stay closed through the ice season.

Where to start

Where do you begin on a chain this big?

Start shallow and start early. The first fishable ice forms in the back bays, which is exactly where the panfish are, so for the first stretch of the season you can have a good day without walking far from shore. As the ice thickens the fishing spreads out to the weed edges and deeper breaks, and by midwinter people are running sleds to main-lake structure.

Good to know

Common questions

When is there usually safe ice on the Sugar Camp Chain?
There is no date you can bank on. The shallow bays skin over first and the main basins follow, once a stretch of cold, calm nights has stacked up enough clear ice. Because the chain is connected by moving water, the narrows and channels lag well behind the bays and can stay dangerous long after the rest looks solid. Check conditions locally the week you come, and never take someone else's tracks as proof.
What fish can you catch through the ice on the Sugar Camp Chain?
Panfish are the bread and butter — bluegill, crappie and perch — and they are the reason most families love first ice here. Walleye come on at dawn and dusk along the deeper weed edges and breaklines, and northern pike will take a tip-up set over a shallow bay all day long. Musky season is closed under the ice, so the chain's headliner gets the winter off.
Do I need my own gear to ice fish near Eagle River?
You can bring everything or almost nothing. Eagle River has bait and tackle shops that will sell you jigs, waxies and minnows and tell you what has been working, and local guides run full-service trips with shacks, heaters and electronics included — worth it for a first time out on water this large. A Wisconsin fishing licence is required year-round.

December in the Northwoods

First snow, early ice on the Sugar Camp Chain, and the quietest holiday weeks you’ll find anywhere.

JournalArea GuideWhere to EatFishingThe Cabins

December is the month the Northwoods changes hands. The fall crowds are long gone, the lakes are still making up their minds, and for a few weeks Eagle River belongs almost entirely to the people who live here. Then the snow settles in, the trails open, and the whole place turns loud and bright again for the holidays. It is a strange, wonderful month to visit.

What to expect

What is December actually like up here?

Think of December as two different months stitched together. The first couple of weeks are the in-between: cold enough that the ground is hard and the bays are skinning over, but not always snowy enough for the sleds. If what you want is a quiet cabin, a fire, and nobody else on the road, this is the best stretch of the winter.

By the back half of the month the Northwoods usually looks the way you picture it — snow in the pines, plowed banks along the county highways, ice shanties starting to appear on the bigger bays. Pack for real winter either way — boots, layers and a warm hat matter more here than anything else you’ll bring, and our packing guide runs through the rest.

On the water

When does the ice come in?

Freeze-up is the thing everyone asks about, and the honest answer is that it happens in stages. The shallow, sheltered bays go first, then the smaller lakes, and the big open water on the Eagle River Chain holds out longest. The Sugar Camp Chain tends to lock up in that same early-winter window, and watching it happen from a warm cabin window is a fine way to spend a morning.

What matters is that early ice is never uniform. Narrows, inlets and spring holes stay soft long after the rest looks solid, and a lake that carried someone yesterday is not a promise about today. Nobody local walks out on first ice without checking first, and neither should you. Our ice-up guide goes deeper on how the chain freezes and who to ask, and once the ice is established the ice fishing here is worth the wait.

Things to do

What is there to do in the meantime?

Walk the woods

Before deep snow arrives, the hiking trails around town are easy going and completely empty. Once it piles up, strap on snowshoes and keep going.

Wait on the trails

Eagle River is a snowmobile town to its bones. Trails open when there is enough base and the ground is frozen — see our snowmobiling guide and watch the local reports.

Sauna and hot tub

Short days are what these were built for. Sit in the cedar barrel sauna until the cold stops mattering, then get in the hot tub and watch your breath.

Dinner in town

Supper clubs and the Friday fish fry are at their best in winter, when the dining rooms are warm and full of locals. Our where-to-eat guide has our regulars.

The holidays

Is a Northwoods cabin a good place to spend Christmas?

We think it is the best version of a holiday there is. Everybody ends up in the same room, there is nowhere to rush off to, and the day stretches out the way holidays are supposed to. Bring a tree if you want one, or don’t — there are several hundred already outside the window. The practical notes: plan on one proper grocery run when you arrive, and remember that small-town places often keep shorter hours around the holidays, so call ahead. The area guide covers what else is nearby.

If you are driving up from the south, give yourself extra time and read our winter driving guide first — the last hour is the one that surprises people.

Good to know

Common questions

Is there enough snow in Eagle River in December?
Usually some, but it builds through the month rather than arriving all at once. Early December can be bare ground or a few inches; by late December the Northwoods is normally in proper winter. Snowmobile and ski trails open once there is enough base and the ground is frozen, so check the local trail reports before you count on riding.
When is the ice safe to walk on?
No early ice is ever guaranteed safe, and the lakes around Eagle River freeze unevenly — narrows, springs and current keep soft spots open long after the bays look solid. Locals wait for several inches of hard, clear ice and still check conditions locally before stepping out. Ask at a bait shop in town and trust that over how the lake looks from shore.
Are the cabins open over the holidays?
Both cabins are open year-round, including Christmas and New Year weeks. Those two weeks are the busiest of the winter, so they book earlier than the rest of the season — if you have a date in mind, reserve well ahead.

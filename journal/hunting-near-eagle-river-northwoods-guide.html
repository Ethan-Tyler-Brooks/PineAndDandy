Northwoods Journal · Fall

Hunting near Eagle River

Grouse in the aspen, deer in the big woods, and public forest in every direction — how to plan a Northwoods hunting trip.

JournalArea GuideWhere to EatFishingThe Cabins

Come fall, the Northwoods changes hands. The pontoons come off the lifts, the traffic thins, and blaze orange shows up at the gas pumps and the breakfast counter. Hunting near Eagle River is one of the oldest reasons people come up here — and the sheer amount of open ground takes some getting used to. Here's how we'd plan the trip.

The calendar

When is hunting season in the Northwoods?

Wisconsin's fall seasons stack on top of each other rather than running one at a time. Archery and crossbow deer and the ruffed grouse season both open in early fall and run long — grouse well past the leaf drop, archery deer into winter. Waterfowl follows as the flights come through. The one everybody circles is the traditional nine-day gun deer season, which runs the week of Thanksgiving.

Exact dates, zones and bag limits are set each year by the Wisconsin DNR, so pull up the current regulations before you book. We'd rather send you to the source than guess.

Where to go

Is there public land to hunt near Eagle River?

An enormous amount of it — the real draw of this corner of the state. You don't need to know a landowner to have a good week up here.

Nicolet National Forest

The Nicolet half of the Chequamegon-Nicolet begins just east of town — mile after mile of mixed hardwood, aspen and swamp conifer, open to hunting under federal and state rules.

County forest land

Vilas and Oneida county forests cover a huge share of the remaining ground, laced with gravel roads and old logging two-tracks that make walking in straightforward.

Young aspen cuts

For birds, forget the postcard forest and find the ugly stuff: five-to-fifteen-year-old aspen regrowth, thick as a hedge. That's where grouse live.

Pick a road on the map, park at a gate, and walk into legal cover. Bring a paper map or download the area offline — cell service thins out fast off the highway.

Birds

What's the grouse hunting like?

Ruffed grouse are the signature bird of the Northwoods, and hunting them is more walking than shooting. Populations here follow a long natural cycle, so some autumns are famously good and others are quiet — but the cover is always there. Early season means leaves still on and birds flushing unseen; by late October the woods open up and the shooting gets fair. Woodcock move through the alder bottoms on their way south, often in a single dramatic week.

A dog helps enormously, and so does a willingness to push into the thick young growth instead of the pretty trail beside it. Expect to earn every bird.

Deer

What should I know about deer hunting up here?

Northern forest deer hunting is a different animal from the farm-country hunting most people picture. Densities are lower, the woods are bigger, and the deer use all of it. Hunters who do well scout hard — funnels between swamps, oak ridges, the edges of recent cuts — then sit patiently in cold that means business. A big-woods buck is genuinely earned, and locals will tell you so.

Blaze orange or blaze pink is required during the gun deer seasons. If you're up here in November for the tail end of fall and not hunting, wear some anyway on the trails — everybody shares these woods.

Make it a trip

How do you build a hunting weekend around it?

The best hunting trips up here are half woods, half cabin. Hunt the morning, come back for a hot shower and a nap, then head out for the evening sit. A Friday fish fry or supper-club dinner is practically obligatory — our where-to-eat guide has the local favorites marked — and the fall fishing is often excellent as the water cools, so a rod in the truck is never wasted. Good to know

Common questions

When is hunting season near Eagle River, Wisconsin?
Archery and crossbow deer and the ruffed grouse season open in early fall, waterfowl follows, and the traditional nine-day gun deer season runs the week of Thanksgiving. Exact dates are set each year by the Wisconsin DNR, so check the current regulations before you book.
Is there public land to hunt near Eagle River?
A great deal of it. The Nicolet side of the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest sits right east of town, and Vilas and Oneida county forest lands cover much of the rest. A visiting hunter can walk in almost anywhere without knocking on a single door.
Do I need a Wisconsin license to hunt as a non-resident?
Yes. Non-residents can buy Wisconsin small game and deer licenses online through the DNR or at license agents in town, and first-time hunters generally need proof of hunter education. Blaze orange or blaze pink is required during the gun deer seasons.
